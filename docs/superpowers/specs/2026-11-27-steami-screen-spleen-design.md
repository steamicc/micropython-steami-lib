# Spleen fonts on the V1c screen in MicroPython — design

Status: rulings of the maintainer and the controller, 2026-11-27. Issue #461.

Shared rules (faces, metrics, layout, accents, storage): the Arduino design
`2026-11-26-steami-display-v1c-font-design.md` of arduino-steami-lib (main at
`64b8db6`, #277), sections 5.1 to 5.5 and 8.1. This note does not copy them; it says how
`micropython-steami-lib` applies them, what it keeps different, and what was decided for MicroPython.
In this note, "5.x" and "8.1" mean sections of that design.

## Intent

Since v0.30.0 the V1c screen (GC9A01, 240 px, k = 2) draws every text with the 8x8 framebuf font
scaled ×2 or ×4 pixel by pixel. The text is blocky, accents are checkerboards, and the per-pixel
`draw_scaled_text` is slow (about 150 ms for the text of program 04). Arduino now draws the V1c text with
the Spleen 2.1.0 faces (6x12, 8x16, 12x24 and 16x32). This project gives MicroPython the same faces, the
same cells and the same positions on 240 px, from the same generated tables, so that a sketch and a
MicroPython program look alike on a V1c. The 128 px panel (V1a/V1b) keeps its 8x8 font.

## Goals

- `steami_screen/spleen.py`, generated by arduino-steami-lib's `scripts/gen-spleen-fonts.py
  --micropython` at commit `64b8db6`, committed unchanged and frozen with `package("steami_screen")`.
- On 240 px, every widget text and the pixel text `text(s, (x, y), c, scale)` drawn in the faces of 5.1
  with the metrics and rules of 5.2 to 5.4 (the rulings below).
- Native faces blitted from the frozen bytes; replicated faces drawn by runs. No per-pixel call.
- Accents drawn on 240 px; folded to one 8x8 cell on 128 px, with a real 8x8 degree sign.
- `Screen.text_height(scale=1)` (the Arduino `textHeight()`), and `max_chars` 30 on 240 px.
- The firmware flash layout that makes room for the tables (fork PR, merged first).

## Non-goals

- Pixel parity of anything but the pixel text (arcs, triangles, trigonometry stay MicroPython's).
- Any change to the drivers' own `text()` (8x8, both drivers) or to the SSD1327 driver.
- Panels other than 128 and 240 px. The Spleen path runs when k > 1, and assumes k = 2.
- The wiki (a later PR). Arduino-side changes.

## Anti-goals

- A 128 px regression: every existing entry of the 128 golden stays byte-identical. The only new 128
  output is the accent fold and the degree sign, pinned by one new golden entry.
- Touching `spleen`, `GS4_HMSB` or `blit` on the 128 path (the 128 golden's `RawDisplay` has neither).
- A widget text slower than today's, or a native-face widget text over 10 ms on the board.
- Hand edits to `spleen.py`.

## Rulings (binding)

Faces and layout on 240 px (k = 2), as 5.1 to 5.3:
- TEXT is 8x16 × f/2; LABEL is 6x12 × 1, with a cell height of 12. VALUE uses a ladder: f 2 is 8x16, f 3 is
  12x24, f 4 is 16x32, and replicated faces above. Bold (a double strike) applies only on 12x24 and 16x32.
- In VALUE ink, '.' and ':' are narrow on every face: window `[w/4, 3w/4)` and advance `w/2`.
- The value fit lowers f down to k and skips each f that has no face.
- Title cap and floor: every 240 title sits on row 37 and is cut at its ink-row chord.
- Compass and watch letters are 8x16 cells centred on the same points as today.
- `max_chars = width // advance(TEXT, k)`: 16 on 128 px, 30 on 240 px.
- The pixel text is 8x16 × scale, with the same pixels as Arduino's `text(s, x, y, c, scale)`. Accents
  come from `EXTRA`.

Drawing:
- Native faces (n = 1), bold included, are blitted from the read-only tuple
  `(memoryview(data)[o:o + h*bpr], w, h, MONO_HLSB)`.
  - The palette is a 2x1 `GS4_HMSB` FrameBuffer: entry 1 = c, entry 0 = b ≠ c. The key is b.
  - Bold is a second blit at x + 1.
  - A narrow glyph is the whole glyph blitted at x − w/4.
- Replicated faces (n ≥ 2) use one `fill_rect` per run of set bits. Bold widens each run by one font
  column. There is never one call per pixel.

APIs:
- The gc9a01 driver's `blit` gains a positional `palette` argument (the C `blit` takes no keywords).
- `GC9A01Display` gains a glyph method that resolves the colour once.
- Framebuf stub:
  - its constructor rejects read-only buffers with `TypeError`;
  - blit sources and palettes given as tuples stay read-only-OK, through an internal helper;
  - its signatures are positional-only, like C.
- `spleen` is imported lazily, on the 240 path only.

128 px (V1a/V1b):
- The 8x8 framebuf font stays.
- Codes 128..159 fold through `FOLD` to one ASCII byte before `framebuf.text`.
- '°' is drawn with Arduino's 8x8 degree glyph (`SteamiFont.cpp` glyph 127). The text is split at '°' before
  folding, so a literal `"\x7f"` is never taken for a degree.
- Nothing else changes: control characters, `"\x7f"` and non-ASCII characters outside `EXTRA` reach
  `framebuf.text` as before (one checkerboard per UTF-8 byte). Only 240 px draws them as one '?' cell.
- One new 128 golden entry pins this change, and its docstring justifies it. Every other entry stays the
  same.

Kept MicroPython differences (listed, not fixed):
- The graph keeps three labels with "k" formatting. On 240 px they use the 6x12 LABEL face, with rows from
  the ink edges as in 5.3.
- The menu keeps its fixed bar and 5 visible rows on 240 px.
- The gauge fill is 2 px thicker.
- The value label is lowered when no character fits.
- The `_at` margins come from real glyph heights.
- The gauge outer radius is `radius − u(4)`.
- The face cell is `W·11/128`.

Firmware (`steamicc/micropython-steami`, branch from `origin/stm32-steami-rev1d-final`):
- A STeaMi-specific `boards/STEAM32_WB55RG/stm32wb55xg_steami.ld`:
  - FLASH 528K, FLASH_APP 496K, FLASH_FS at 0x08084000 with 256K;
  - ld `ASSERT`s, including FS end ≤ 0x080E0000.
- `LD_FILES` names that script for the plain and the mboot builds.
- `rfcore_firmware.py` `STAGING_AREA_START` moves from 0x80C0000 to 0x80C4000. Otherwise a radio-stack update
  would erase the moved file system's last 16 KB. The staging window shrinks to 112 KB (BLE_HCILayer v1.20.0
  is 80 KB); a larger image goes through STM32CubeProgrammer or EasyFlash.
- Every firmware build clears `frozen_mpy`, `frozen_content.c` and `firmware.elf` first: the frozen `.mpy`
  cache is keyed on mtimes and the elf does not depend on the linker script.
- The update reformats the file system, so user files are lost. The release notes say so.
- The mboot build already fails to link (pre-existing): noted, not fixed.
- The fork PR merges before the lib PR, or the lib's release build overflows.

## Approaches

1. **Blit native faces, run-fill replicated faces, glyph loop in the adapter (chosen).** It needs about
   31 C-level blits for program 04, and the colour is resolved once per string. The frozen bytes stay in
   flash, with no copy.
2. **A row drawer for every face.** It is simpler: one path. But a native 8x16 glyph costs 10 to 16
   `fill_rect` calls instead of one blit. That is too slow for the 10 ms bound (about 1 ms of Python per
   row-level call measured through the adapter).
3. **Copy each glyph into a reused writable FrameBuffer per face size, then blit.** It works on the board,
   but costs a copy per glyph and one buffer per size, for no gain over the read-only tuple.
4. **`spleen.mpy` on the file system** (8.1 option (b)). The maintainer set it aside: it costs heap, adds
   an installation step lost at each reformat, and needs an 8x8 fallback.

## Design

**`steami_screen/fonts.py` (new, pure Python).** It imports neither `framebuf` nor `spleen` at import
time. It holds:
- the ink constants `TEXT`, `LABEL` and `VALUE`;
- the code mapping (rule of 5.6) and the 128 fold;
- the face ladder (it imports `spleen` inside `face()` only, once, and keeps the module in a global);
- advances, widths (with prefix widths in one pass), prefix ink masks (each glyph's row mask cached under a
  small int key) and ink rows;
- the run drawer `draw_runs(fr, fc, glyphs, n, bold, c)`, the one place the run algorithm lives;
- `DEGREE8`, the 8x8 degree as a face tuple shaped like the Spleen ones.

The 128 fold strings are a copy of `spleen.EXTRA` and `spleen.FOLD`, with '°' mapped to code 127. A test
pins the copy to the module, so the 128 path never imports `spleen`.

**`GC9A01Display.draw_glyphs(fc, glyphs, n, bold, color)`.** It resolves the colour once:
- at n = 1, it blits each `(offset, x, y)` entry (twice when bold) with the shared 2x1 palette and key;
- at n ≥ 2, it calls `fonts.draw_runs` with the raw `fill_rect`.

The palette and the blit target are created lazily, on the first call.

**Measured on the board (deviation).** On the V1c (STM32WB55 at 64 MHz, MicroPython 1.29) the C palette blit
costs 1.2 ms per 16x32 glyph (2.2 ms bold, two blits), so widget text took 10 to 25 ms, not 10 ms. Native
glyphs, and replicated ones too, are now written by a viper function instead of palette blits:
- `_write_glyph` (`@micropython.viper`, module level in `gc9a01.py`) writes a glyph's set bits straight into
  the driver's `GS4_HMSB` frame (`raw.buffer`), one call per glyph at any n; bold ORs each row with itself
  one column right in the same pass;
- every write is clipped to the frame, since viper has no bounds checks;
- `draw_glyphs` resolves the frame and its size once per display; a raw without a whole `GS4_HMSB` frame
  keeps the blit and `fonts.draw_runs`.

The pixels are identical: the mock tests compare the frame byte for byte with the blits (n = 1) and the
`fill_rect` runs (n ≥ 2), clipped edges included. The board prototype took 0.47 to 0.50 ms per 16x32 glyph,
bold included, against 1.2 ms for the blit. Program 04's text is 28 writer calls (the bold value is one call
per glyph, not two blits).

**`show()` speed-up (maintainer request).** The maintainer asked to speed up the driver's `show()` in this
PR, after the two tips of the stm32python round TFT page (the highest SPI clock, native or viper code).
Measured by the controller on the V1c (STM32WB55 at 64 MHz, MicroPython 1.29, real driver), `show()` took
167 ms: 15 strips of 16 rows, each a palette blit from `GS4_HMSB` to RGB565 (7.1 ms) and an SPI write
(3.9 ms at 16 MHz). A viper converter gave byte-identical strips in 3.6 ms per strip:
- `_to_rgb565` (`@micropython.viper`, module level in `gc9a01/device.py`) looks up each nibble's RGB565 word
  in a 32-byte palette buffer built once in `__init__` by an RGB565 FrameBuffer, so the words are copied in
  the byte order the blit wrote (panel order);
- it converts the rows of one strip only, the last short strip included; an odd width's padding nibble
  gives no pixel;
- the mock tests compare the SPI writes byte for byte with the old blit, on guarded buffers (the
  micropython stub views ptr16 arguments as little-endian words), and the host unix MicroPython ran the
  real viper function with the same output.

The SPI clock goes from 16 to 32 MHz (`BAUDRATE` in `gc9a01/const.py`, the SPI1 maximum: 64 MHz PCLK2 / 2),
measured by the controller at 2.0 ms per strip instead of 3.9 ms, so `show()` should take about
15 x (3.6 + 2.0) = 85 ms. The maintainer's rule: if the panel does not hold 32 MHz, keep 16 MHz. The
controller checks it on the board under the webcam; the change is one constant in its own commit, easy to
revert.

**`Screen` (device.py).** When k > 1:
- `_fit` and `_put` take an ink. The face is judged once from the whole text, and `_fit` returns it with
  the fit, so a cut prefix is drawn in the face of the whole text. The prefix masks, the replication and
  the widths come from that face, each computed in one pass. `_place` draws a fit already computed:
  `_value` fits the value and its label once and places them without fitting again. `_put` keeps at most
  10 parameters (the repo's ruff limit), so `pad` stays with `_value`.
- `_draw` builds the glyph entries (narrow offset, advance, bold flag) and calls `draw_glyphs`. A display
  without that method gets `fonts.draw_runs` over `Screen._fill_rect`.
- `_txt` draws through `_draw` with the ink the caller names.

When k = 1, a new `_text8` folds the string and splits it at the degree sign. It draws the ASCII segments
exactly as before, and each degree with `draw_runs` over `DEGREE8` (six `fill_rect` calls at scale 1). Added:
`text_height()`, `max_chars` from the TEXT advance, and the title cap from 13 TEXT advances (52 px of half
width).

## Testing

- `tests/test_steami_screen_spleen_mock.py`:
  - the module contract (sha256 of the committed file);
  - the code mapping and fold;
  - the ladder;
  - widths and masks;
  - the run drawer;
  - pixel parity of `text()` against `spleen.py` rows, with accents;
  - call-count guards;
  - the 128 path without `spleen`.
- `tests/test_steami_screen_scaling_mock.py`: the 240 numbers come from the Arduino goldens
  (`test_main.cpp` and `golden_layout.py` at main `64b8db6`), except where a kept difference applies.
- Framebuf stub tests, the driver blit palette test and adapter tests.
- The new 128 golden entry.
- V1c scenario additions, run by the controller:
  - `spleen` is frozen;
  - `FrameBuffer(bytes)` raises `TypeError`;
  - accents are drawn;
  - timing with `time.ticks_us` of the 8.1 list (title, value with unit and label, subtitle and gauge
    labels of programs 01 and 04, a 15-character `text(s, (x, y), c, 2)`), after a warm-up, with
    `gc.collect()` before each call and the minimum of 3 runs, on the old and the new firmware;
  - `max_chars == 30`.
